# The 30-minute biz case — for pitch-heavy events

Some hackathons (corporate, Devpost challenges, "startup in a weekend" formats) weight *opportunity* over engineering. If the rubric has market/business/impact axes, spend 30 minutes here — it beats 3 extra hours of polish on a product judges score as a toy.

## When this applies

- Rubric has explicit business/market/impact/viability criteria, OR
- Judge mix from `recon.py` skews investor/founder, OR
- The event is a challenge prize ("solve X for sponsor Y") where the winner must read as a product, not a demo.

If the rubric is pure technical + demo, skip this file — spend the 30 minutes on reliability.

## The four artifacts (30 min total, agent does drafts)

1. **One-persona one-pain** (5 min). Not "developers" or "small businesses" — a specific human with a specific recurring pain. "On-call engineer who gets paged for alerts that turn out to be noise" beats "DevOps teams". Every judge question about "who's the user" routes through this persona.
2. **TAM back-of-envelope** (10 min). One number, one assumption chain, said in one sentence: "~2M on-call engineers globally × $50/yr tool budget ≈ $100M". Judges don't verify the math — they check that you can think in numbers. Never say "the market is huge".
3. **The data story** (10 min). One sentence connecting the pain to something measurable: "the average on-call engineer burns 4h/week on false alerts" — cite a survey, a stat, or your own lived number. A pitch with one real number beats a pitch with ten adjectives.
4. **One comparable + one difference** (5 min). "PagerDuty does this at $41/user/mo for enterprises; we're the $5 version for indie teams." Judges ask "how is this different from X" — the answer lives here, rehearsed.

## Wiring it into the pitch

- The persona opens the demo script ("this is Priya, she got paged 40 times last week").
- The TAM sentence + comparable slide into the *future* beat, not the opening — demo first, market second.
- Every artifact goes in the Devpost submission text. Async judges score from the page, not the room.

## Anti-patterns

- Inventing statistics — judges ask "where's that from" and a shrug costs more than no number.
- Full market analysis / SWOT / personas-with-stock-photos — that's a consultant deliverable, and it's the kind of depth that signals you didn't build.
- Pricing debates during the event — pick a number, move on.
