# Booth/table judging — the format that actually decides most hackathons

Science-fair style: judges rotate to your table, loud room, 3-5 minutes, no stage. Different game from the 90-second pitch.

## The setup (done before judging opens)

- Laptop open, demo loaded and warmed up, brightness max, notifications off.
- One visual always up: a single slide or the app's home screen. Judges can't hear in loud rooms — the screen carries the pitch. 4 of 5 teams fail with nothing on screen.
- Appendix tabs ready: architecture diagram, sponsor API proof, metrics. Answer deep questions by opening a tab, not by describing.

## The 3-minute arc

1. **0-15s — hook before touching the demo.** Two sentences: "X people waste Y. We built Z — watch." Then hands to keyboard.
2. **15s-2min — one journey, narrated.** Same walk as demo-script.md, but slower — they interrupt, that's good.
3. **Last minute — invite the rubric.** "The interesting part technically is…" steer them to your strongest criterion.

## Reading the judge

- Investor-type at your table → spend the last minute on "who pays / why now".
- Engineer → open the appendix, show the hard part, invite code questions.
- Sponsor engineer → name their API in the first 30 seconds and show it working.

## Never say

- "That's out of scope" — say "next step is X" and move on.
- "It's just a prototype" — you built the thing; own it.
- "Sorry about the wifi" — have the video/GIF fallback cued before they arrive.

## If the demo is dead

Show the video. Judges score what they see working — a clean video of a working thing beats a live demo of a broken one.
